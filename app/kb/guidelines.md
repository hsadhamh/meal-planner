# Knowledge base: nutrition guidelines (POC)

Each section is one retrievable chunk. Keep a `Source:` line on every chunk; the agent cites it.

## Adult protein and energy requirements (ICMR-NIN 2020)
ICMR-NIN 2020 sets the protein RDA for Indian adults at 0.83 g per kg body weight a day: about 54 g for a 65 kg man and 46 g for a 55 kg woman. Energy for sedentary work is about 2,110 kcal for men and 1,660 kcal for women; moderate activity is 2,710 and 2,130 kcal. This planner treats the RDA as a floor and aims for about 75 g protein for adult men and 60 g for adult women on lower-carb plates.
Source: https://www.nin.res.in/rdabook/brief_note.pdf

## Breastfeeding mother requirements (ICMR-NIN 2020)
A breastfeeding mother needs extra energy and protein: +600 kcal and +16.9 g protein a day for months 0-6, and +520 kcal and +13.2 g protein a day for months 7-12. She should not follow a low-carb plan; keep a normal grain portion plus one extra protein serving and one extra snack.
Source: https://www.nin.res.in/rdabook/brief_note.pdf

## Children's requirements (ICMR-NIN 2020)
Protein RDA for children: 12.5 g a day at 1-3 years, 15.9 g at 4-6 years and 23.3 g at 7-9 years. Energy: 1,070 kcal at 1-3 years, 1,360 kcal at 4-6 years and 1,700 kcal at 7-9 years. Children should keep full grain portions; do not apply adult carb reduction to children.
Source: https://www.nin.res.in/rdabook/brief_note.pdf

## Cooking oil limit (ICMR-NIN 2020)
Visible fat or cooking oil should be limited to 20-50 g (4 to 10 teaspoons) per person per day depending on energy needs, and not more than 27 g for a 2,000 kcal diet.
Source: https://www.nin.res.in/rdabook/brief_note.pdf

## My Plate for the Day (ICMR-NIN)
For a 2,000 kcal day ICMR-NIN's plate suggests 250 g cereals and millets, 85 g pulses (eggs, fish or meat can replace part), 300 ml milk or curd, 400 g vegetables including greens, 100 g fruit, 35 g nuts and seeds, and 27 g fats and oils. Fresh fruit is preferred over juice.
Source: https://www.nin.res.in/downloads/My%20plate%20for%20the%20day%20A4.pdf

## Cereal share and whole grains (ICMR Dietary Guidelines 2024)
ICMR's 2024 Dietary Guidelines for Indians say cereals should provide no more than 45% of total energy, at least half of cereals should be whole grains and millets, pulses, eggs and meat should provide 14-15% of energy, and fat should be limited to 30% of energy. Protein supplements are not recommended for muscle building when the diet is balanced.
Source: https://www.dailypioneer.com/2024/columnists/key-takeaways-from-icmr---s-new-dietary-guidelines.html

## Typical Indian diet is carb-heavy (ICMR-INDIAB)
The ICMR-INDIAB study (Nature Medicine, 2025) found Indians get about 62% of daily energy from carbohydrates and only about 12% from protein, largely from white rice, milled grains and added sugar. Researchers recommend lowering total carbohydrate and getting more energy from plant and dairy protein; swapping white rice for millets alone is not enough.
Source: https://health.medicaldialogues.in/health-topics/diabetes-health/icmr-study-finds-dietary-imbalance-behind-obesity-and-diabetes-surge-156246

## Dietary fibre (NIN 2020)
NIN's 2020 report recommends about 40 g of dietary fibre per 2,000 kcal: about 40 g for a man, 33 g for a woman and 27 g for a 4-6 year old child. Fibre comes from vegetables twice a day, a legume daily, millets and red or brown rice, and whole fruit.
Source: https://fit.thequint.com/chew-on-this/five-kgs-added-to-ideal-weight-of-indians-65-for-men-60-for-women-nin

## Complementary feeding frequency (WHO)
WHO advises complementary foods 2-3 times a day at 6-8 months and 3-4 times a day at 9-11 months, alongside continued breastfeeding. Pureed, mashed and semi-solid foods from 6 months; most infants manage finger foods from about 8 months. Include meat, poultry, fish or eggs as often as possible.
Source: https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/child-health-recommended-food-for-the-very-early-years

## Complementary feeding amounts and oil (UNICEF)
From 6 up to 9 months: mashed food 2-3 times a day plus 1-2 snacks, 2-3 tablespoons up to half a cup (125 ml) per meal. From 9 up to 12 months: 3-4 times a day plus 1-2 snacks, at least half a cup per meal. Infants need only a very small amount of added oil, no more than half a teaspoon a day. Avoid tea, coffee, soda and sugary drinks.
Source: https://www.unicef.org/media/108431/file/Brochure%20Feeding%20After%206%20Months.pdf

## Foods to avoid for babies (NHS)
Do not give honey before 12 months (infant botulism). Do not add salt or sugar to a baby's food or cooking water. Whole nuts are a choking risk under 5; crushed or ground nuts can be given from around 6 months. Avoid shark, swordfish and marlin. Eggs can be given from around 6 months; non-lion-mark eggs should be cooked until white and yolk are solid.
Source: https://www.nhs.uk/baby/weaning-and-feeding/foods-to-avoid-giving-babies-and-young-children/

## Drinks for babies (NHS)
From around 6 months offer sips of water from a cup with meals. Continue breast milk or first infant formula until at least 1 year. Cows' milk can be used in cooking from 6 months but not as a main drink until 12 months. Babies under 12 months do not need fruit juice or smoothies. Tea and coffee are not suitable for babies or young children.
Source: https://www.nhs.uk/baby/weaning-and-feeding/drinks-and-cups-for-babies-and-young-children/

## Bread and finger foods for babies (NHS)
Starchy foods suitable from around 6 months include bread, toast, pitta, chapatti, rice, oats and porridge; they can be offered as finger foods. Cook vegetables soft. Babies do not need salt or sugar added and should not eat salty foods.
Source: https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/baby/weaning/what-to-feed-your-baby/from-around-6-months/

## Salt limits by age (nidirect)
Babies under 1 year need less than 1 g of salt a day. Maximum salt: 2 g a day at 1-3 years, 3 g at 4-6 years, 5 g at 7-10 years, 6 g from 11 years and for adults. Bread products can be high in salt.
Source: https://www.nidirect.gov.uk/articles/salt-0

## Foods and drinks to avoid or limit for infants (CDC)
Do not give honey before 12 months. Cow's milk before 12 months may cause intestinal bleeding. Children younger than 12 months should not drink any fruit or vegetable juice. Children under 24 months should not have added sugars or caffeinated drinks. Avoid fish high in mercury and unpasteurized foods.
Source: https://www.cdc.gov/infant-toddler-nutrition/foods-and-drinks/foods-and-drinks-to-avoid-or-limit.html

## Choking hazards (CDC)
Choking hazards for infants and toddlers include whole or chopped nuts and seeds, spoonfuls of nut butter, whole beans, whole corn kernels, uncut grapes and cherry tomatoes, hard raw carrot or apple, popcorn, breads with seeds or whole-grain kernels, tough meat and bones in meat or fish. Cut and mash food to the child's stage, have them sit upright, and watch them while they eat.
Source: https://www.cdc.gov/infant-toddler-nutrition/foods-and-drinks/choking-hazards.html

## Fish choices and children's servings (FDA/EPA)
Best choices (low mercury) include Atlantic and chub mackerel, sardine, anchovy, tilapia, shrimp and canned light tuna. Avoid king mackerel, shark, swordfish, marlin and bigeye tuna. Children 1-11 can eat 2 servings a week from best choices: about 28 g at 1-3 years, 55 g at 4-7 years, 85 g at 8-10 years. In South Indian markets, ayala (Indian mackerel), mathi (sardine), nethili (anchovy), vavval (pomfret) and prawns are common lower-mercury picks; keep seer (vanjaram) and tuna as adult fish.
Source: https://fda.gov/media/102331/download

## Allergen introduction for babies (planner rule)
Introduce one new allergenic food at a time (egg, peanut, sesame, fish, wheat, dairy, tree nuts), in the morning, and wait a few days before the next. With a family history of food allergy, ask a doctor before introducing nuts. Introduce peanut, almond and sesame individually before using a mixed nut-seed powder.
Source: https://www.nhs.uk/baby/weaning-and-feeding/foods-to-avoid-giving-babies-and-young-children/

## Lower-carb South Indian adult plate (planner rule)
Adult plate: half vegetables (double poriyal or kootu plus salad), a quarter protein at every meal (2 eggs, fish or chicken, paneer, or 1.5 katori dal or sundal), a quarter grain at most (2 idli, 1 dosa, 1 slice multigrain bread or 1 small katori rice). Dinner grain is optional. Cook grain and dal in separate cooker containers on pongal and khichdi nights so adults can take mostly dal and vegetables. One fried snack a week.
Source: planner design (Ore Samayal POC)

## Prep overlap (planner rule)
One fermented batter covers 2-3 breakfasts (idli, then dosa, then uthappam) and keeps 3 days in the fridge. Cook each pulse pot once for a snack sundal and a next-day kuzhambu; cooked pulses keep 2 days. Cook meat once for lunch and turn the reserved portion into a dry pepper fry for dinner. Lift the baby's and children's portions before salt, chilli and tempering.
Source: planner design (Ore Samayal POC)

## Medical conditions (safety rule)
For diabetes, heart disease, kidney disease, pregnancy or a weight-loss programme, grain, oil and protein portions should be set by a doctor or dietitian. High-protein plates need a doctor's approval for anyone with kidney disease. The assistant gives general meal-planning guidance, not medical advice.
Source: planner safety policy (Ore Samayal POC)
